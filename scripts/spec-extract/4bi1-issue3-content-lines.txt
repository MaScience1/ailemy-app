[p=17 x=62.3 Verdana-Bold [10]] (b) Variety of living organisms
[p=17 x=62.3 Verdana-Bold [10]] Students should:
[p=17 x=62.3 Verdana [10]] 1.2 describe the common features shown by eukaryotic organisms: plants, animals, fungi
[p=17 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] and protoctists
[p=17 x=62.3 Verdana [10]]  Plants: these are multicellular organisms; their cells contain chloroplasts and are able
[p=17 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] to carry out photosynthesis; their cells have cellulose cell walls; they store
[p=17 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] carbohydrates as starch or sucrose. Examples include flowering plants, such as a
[p=17 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] cereal (for example, maize), and a herbaceous legume (for example, peas or beans).
[p=17 x=62.3 Verdana [10]]  Animals: these are multicellular organisms; their cells do not contain chloroplasts and
[p=17 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] are not able to carry out photosynthesis; they have no cell walls; they usually have
[p=17 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] nervous co-ordination and are able to move from one place to another; they often
[p=17 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] store carbohydrate as glycogen. Examples include mammals (for example, humans)
[p=17 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] and insects (for example, housefly and mosquito).
[p=17 x=62.3 Verdana [10]]  Fungi: these are organisms that are not able to carry out photosynthesis; their body
[p=17 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] is usually organised into a mycelium made from thread-like structures called hyphae,
[p=17 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] which contain many nuclei; some examples are single-celled; their cells have walls
[p=17 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] made of chitin; they feed by extracellular secretion of digestive enzymes onto food
[p=17 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] material and absorption of the organic products; this is known as saprotrophic
[p=17 x=98.0 Verdana|Verdana-Italic [10]] nutrition; they may store carbohydrate as glycogen. Examples include Mucor, which
[p=17 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] has the typical fungal hyphal structure, and yeast, which is single-celled.
[p=17 x=62.3 Verdana|Verdana-Italic [10]]  Protoctists: these are microscopic single-celled organisms. Some, like  Amoeba, that
[p=17 x=98.0 Verdana|Verdana-Italic [10]] live in pond water, have features like an animal cell, while others, like  Chlorella, have
[p=17 x=98.0 Verdana|Verdana-Italic [10]] chloroplasts and are more like plants. A pathogenic example is Plasmodium,
[p=17 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] responsible for causing malaria.

[p=18 x=62.3 Verdana-Bold [10]] Students should:
[p=18 x=62.3 Verdana [10]] 1.3 describe the common features shown by prokaryotic organisms such as bacteria
[p=18 x=62.3 Verdana [10]]  Bacteria: these are microscopic single-celled organisms; they have a cell wall, cell
[p=18 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] membrane, cytoplasm and plasmids; they lack a nucleus but contain a circular
[p=18 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] chromosome of DNA; some bacteria can carry out photosynthesis but most feed off
[p=18 x=98.0 Verdana|Verdana-Italic [10]] other living or dead organisms. Examples include Lactobacillus bulgaricus, a
[p=18 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] rod-shaped bacterium used in the production of yoghurt from milk, and
[p=18 x=98.0 Verdana|Verdana-Italic [10]] Pneumococcus, a spherical bacterium that acts as the pathogen causing pneumonia.
[p=18 x=62.3 Verdana [10]] 1.4 understand the term pathogen and know that pathogens may include fungi, bacteria,
[p=18 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] protoctists or viruses
[p=18 x=62.3 Verdana [10]]  Viruses: these are not living organisms. They are small particles, smaller than
[p=18 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] bacteria; they are parasitic and can reproduce only inside living cells; they infect
[p=18 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] every type of living organism. They have a wide variety of shapes and sizes; they
[p=18 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] have no cellular structure but have a protein coat and contain one type of nucleic
[p=18 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] acid, either DNA or RNA. Examples include the tobacco mosaic virus that causes
[p=18 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] discolouring of the leaves of tobacco plants by preventing the formation of
[p=18 x=98.0 Verdana [10]] chloroplasts, the influenza virus that causes ‘flu’ and the HIV virus that causes AIDS.
